5 Signs Your Instagram-Only Business Needs a Website

By Emmanuel · WebCraft Nigeria

Instagram got a lot of Nigerian businesses started, and it still works as a discovery and marketing channel. But relying on it as your *entire* online presence starts to show cracks as you grow. Here are five signs it's time to add a real website alongside it.

1. You're answering the same questions dozens of times a day

"How much?" "Do you deliver to my area?" "What sizes do you have?" If you're typing the same answers on repeat, that's information a website page could answer instantly — for every visitor, at any hour.

2. Your best products are buried in old posts

Instagram is a feed, not a catalogue. Anything posted more than a few days ago is effectively invisible unless someone scrolls back through your entire grid. A website keeps everything visible and organized, permanently.

3. New customers hesitate to send money to a page with no website

This is especially true for higher-ticket items — hair, fabric, real estate, gold and luxury goods. A real website signals legitimacy in a way an Instagram page alone often can't, particularly for first-time buyers who don't know you yet.

4. You can't be found on Google at all

Instagram content generally doesn't rank in Google search. If someone searches "shoe vendor in Lagos" or "wedding fabric seller," an Instagram-only business is invisible to that entire pool of potential customers actively looking to buy.

5. You look the same as every other vendor in your niche

Templated bios, similar grid layouts, the same highlight-story structure — most competitors in a given niche end up looking interchangeable on Instagram. A dedicated website, built around your specific brand, is one of the fastest ways to actually stand out.

"Instagram is where people discover you. A website is where they decide to trust you."

The good news

None of this means abandoning Instagram — it still works well for marketing and reach. It just shouldn't be the only place your business exists online. See examples for hair, fabric, bakeries, shoes, and real estate.
